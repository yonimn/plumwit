The short version

  • No accounts, no sign-in. The app never asks for a name, email address, phone number, photo, voice or contacts, and it has no chat or way for players to talk to each other.
  • Puzzles and progress stay on the phone. We can't see them.
  • We treat every player as a child. We don't ask anyone's age. Instead, every ad request from the app is marked as "child-directed", for players of all ages.
  • Ads keep the game free, and they are never personalized. Ads come from Google AdMob: a full-screen ad between some levels, and an optional "Ad hint" that a player can choose to watch. Ads are not based on what a player did in other apps or websites, there is no remarketing, the device's advertising ID is not sent, and ads are limited to Google's "G" (general audiences) rating.
  • No analytics and no crash-reporting services. The app doesn't include Google Analytics for Firebase, Crashlytics or any other analytics service.
  • No in-app purchases.
  • We don't sell personal information, and we don't share it for targeted advertising.

In kid-friendly words

Your puzzles and stars are saved only on your phone. We don't know who you are, and we never ask your name. Tessellogic shows ads so the game can be free. Those ads come from Google, and they are picked to be OK for kids. Google gets some technical information from the phone so it can show an ad, but it isn't allowed to use that information to follow you around with ads. If you have a question, ask a grown-up to email us.

What stays on the device

The game saves on the device: solved levels, progress, stars, daily-puzzle results, tutorial status, your settings, a list of levels you've already rated with the in-game "how fun was it?" step, simple per-level play stats (moves, time, hints) used to show your results, and a counter that keeps ads from appearing too often.

None of this is sent to us or to anyone else. It's deleted when you uninstall the app or clear its storage. The app is set up so that this data is not copied into Android device backups. This means progress doesn't move to a new phone.

Google's ad and privacy-message tools may also store small technical settings on the device, for example to limit how often an ad is shown.

What leaves the device, and who receives it

The game itself doesn't send anything to us. We don't run servers for the app. The only company that receives data from the app is Google, through two Google software components in the app.

1. Ads: Google Mobile Ads SDK (AdMob)

To show an ad, measure it and protect against ad fraud, the Google Mobile Ads SDK sends Google:

  • the device's IP address, which can be used to estimate a general location (about city level) so ads fit the country and language
  • technical device information and app-specific identifiers, such as the device model, Android version, language and an app set ID (an ID that is the same across apps from the same developer on that device). The Android advertising ID is not sent.
  • interactions with ads and the app, such as the app starting, an ad being shown, tapped or closed, and an ad video being watched
  • diagnostic information, such as app launch time, hang rate and energy use

Because every ad request from the app is marked child-directed, Google says it will:

  • not show personalized (interest-based) ads and not use remarketing
  • not send requests to third-party ad vendors, such as ad measurement pixels and third-party ad servers
  • apply its ad-serving protections for children
  • not transmit the Android advertising ID

The ads you see are contextual: they are chosen from what is happening right now (this app, the general location, the language), not from a profile about the player. They are also limited to Google's "G" rating, meaning suitable for general audiences. We use only Google's own ad network in the app, with no other ad networks.

2. Privacy messages: Google User Messaging Platform (UMP) SDK

Each time the app starts, the UMP SDK checks with Google whether a privacy message is needed in the player's region. The app tells it that the player is under the age of consent. Because of that, the UMP SDK doesn't ask for consent and doesn't show a consent form. To answer that check, Google receives technical information such as the IP address and the app's identity.

What we see

We see only aggregated reports in our AdMob account, such as how many ads were shown and how much they earned. We don't see information about individual players, and we can't link anything to a person.

Sharing a daily result

When a player taps Share after a daily puzzle, Android's share menu opens with a short line of text: the date and the stars. The player chooses where it goes. It contains no personal information, and we don't receive it.

Children and parents (US COPPA)

Tessellogic is designed for players of all ages, including children under 13. We don't knowingly collect personal information from children, such as a name, contact details, photos, voice or precise location. We have no accounts, no chat, no social features and no way for a player to publish anything.

Persistent identifiers used only to run the app. The only information collected through the app that the US Children's Online Privacy Protection Act (COPPA) treats as personal information is technical: the IP address and app-specific device identifiers that the Google Mobile Ads SDK collects (see above). They are used only for what COPPA calls "support for the internal operations" of the app:

  • serving contextual ads
  • limiting how often ads are shown (frequency capping) and counting ads in aggregate
  • protecting the security and integrity of the app and the ad system, including detecting ad fraud
  • keeping the app and the ad system working properly

How we make sure these identifiers aren't used for anything else. Every ad request is marked child-directed, which tells Google to switch off personalized advertising, remarketing and third-party ad-vendor requests and not to transmit the advertising ID. The app doesn't include the permission that lets apps read the advertising ID. The app limits ads to the "G" rating. The app contains no analytics or crash-reporting service, and the ad SDK's own app-measurement feature is switched off. We use no other ad network. These identifiers are never used to contact a specific person, build a profile about a person, or show behavioral (interest-based) ads.

Which companies collect information through the app: ONE LANGUAGE G.Y.E LTD (us; contact details above) and Google LLC, 1600 Amphitheatre Parkway, Mountain View, CA 94043, USA, through the Google Mobile Ads SDK and the UMP SDK described above. Please send any questions about children's information to us. We'll answer them and, where needed, pass them to Google.

Where information is processed

We're based in Israel. Google processes data on servers around the world, including in the United States. Google LLC participates in the EU-U.S. Data Privacy Framework, the UK Extension and the Swiss-U.S. Data Privacy Framework. The European Commission recognizes Israel as providing an adequate level of data protection. Where other transfers need safeguards, they rely on the safeguards in Google's terms, such as standard contractual clauses.

Your rights

Everyone: contact us at info@safa1.co.il with any privacy question or request.

Parents in the US: see "Children and parents" above.

EEA, UK and Switzerland: you have the right to access your personal data, correct it, have it erased, restrict or object to its processing (including processing based on legitimate interests), and receive it in a portable format. A parent or guardian can use these rights for their child. You can also complain to your local data protection authority. In the UK that's the ICO.

Israel: under the Protection of Privacy Law, 5741-1981 (as amended, including Amendment 13, in force since 14 August 2025), you have the right to review personal information about you held in a database, in Hebrew, Arabic or English. You can also ask us to correct or delete information that is inaccurate, incomplete, unclear or out of date. Giving us information is voluntary, and the game works without it. You can complain to Israel's Privacy Protection Authority.

United States (state privacy laws): we don't sell personal information, we don't share it for cross-context behavioral advertising, and we don't use it for targeted advertising. You can still ask us what we hold and ask us to delete it. We won't treat anyone differently for using these rights.

How requests work without accounts: because Tessellogic has no accounts and we hold no player-level data, we usually can't link any data to a person. When you write to us, we'll explain what we can do. We reply within the time the law requires, for example one month under the GDPR.

Security

Data sent by the Google SDKs in the app is encrypted in transit (TLS/HTTPS). We don't run our own servers for the app. We collect as little as we can, and only the people who need it can access our Google AdMob account. No system is perfectly secure, but we take reasonable steps to protect information, including a written information security program covering the little information we handle.
